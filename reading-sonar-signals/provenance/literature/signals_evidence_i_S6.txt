VI Experiments VI-A Experimental Setup VI-A 1 Implementation details. For feature extraction, the Log-Mel spectrogram uses an FFT size of 4096 and a hop length of 320 with 128 Mel filters over 10–12,000 Hz; the line spectrum is computed at 1,024 Hz sampling with an FFT size of 4096 over 0.5–300 Hz; the DEMON spectrum is extracted from a 100–6,000 Hz band-passed envelope analyzed at 256 Hz over 0–64 Hz modulation frequency. Non-overlapping 16 × 16 16\times 16 patches are used for the Log-Mel and DEMON representations, while the line spectrum employs 16 × 1 16\times 1 frequency–time patches with a stride of 16 × 2 16\times 2 , yielding 400, 380, and 64 tokens respectively. The encoder backbone is initialized with pretrained AudioMAE ViT-Base weights. The LLM is Qwen3-4B-Instruct, loaded in 4-bit precision and adapted with LoRA of rank 16. VI-A 2 Training protocol. Training proceeds in two stages. In the first stage, UPT is optimized under Eq. (9) for 50 epochs using AdamW with batch size 16; the backbone is frozen for the first three epochs and then unfrozen. In the second stage, the complete UPD framework is optimized under Eq. (3) for 3 epochs with an effective batch size of 8, while the pretrained LLM parameters remain frozen. Both stages use a cosine learning-rate schedule with a warmup ratio of 0.05. All experiments are conducted on a single NVIDIA RTX 4090 GPU. VI-B Evaluation Protocol The evaluation follows the dual granularity of the supervision. For the discrete physical tasks of UPT, classification accuracy is reported for SFE, STE, and BPFE, and Micro-F1 for the multi-label MLD task. For the structured descriptions of UPD, conventional sentence-level text similarity metrics do not reflect the correctness of individual physical attributes. We therefore adopt an attribute-level protocol: each generated response is parsed into individual fields according to the fixed field order. Classification accuracy is used for categorical fields (vessel identity, operating state, shaft-frequency trend); mean absolute error (MAE) and root mean squared error (RMSE) are used for numerical fields (shaft-frequency start/end/mean, BPF values, SNR). For machinery fundamental frequencies, whose order carries no physical meaning, a permutation-invariant matching is applied: a predicted frequency matches a reference if their difference is within 1 Hz, one-to-one assignment is resolved by maximizing matched pairs and then minimizing total frequency error, and precision, recall, and F1 are computed accordingly. This protocol assesses the physical accuracy of individual attributes rather than relying on the overall linguistic similarity of the generated descriptions.
