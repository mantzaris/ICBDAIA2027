VI Experiments This section specifies the implementation and evaluates target presence, bearing, spectral lines, received SNR, and horizontal range under white noise and a mixed background. VI-A Experimental Setup VI-A 1 Implementation Details The branches use the shared observation windows defined in Section IV-B . Table IV lists their internal Fourier settings. All transforms use periodic Hann windows. Each DOA observation window contains J = 39 J=39 spectral snapshots. The bearing grid has N θ = 360 N_{\theta}=360 positions, from 0 ∘ 0^{\circ} to 359 ∘ 359^{\circ} in 1 ∘ 1^{\circ} steps. The LINE grid contains N f = 2001 N_{f}=2001 frequency bins over 0–1,000 Hz. LoRA uses rank 8, an alpha parameter of 16, and dropout 0.05 in the query and value projections. The 201 × 201 201\times 201 bathymetric grid is mapped to 16 tokens; receiver metadata is supplied as text. Including the additional frequency-readout position, the multimodal prefix contains 169 nontext positions plus instruction and metadata tokens. The answer follows the readout position. Computation uses NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPUs with 24 GB memory. Spectral and spatial statistics use FP32, and language adaptation uses BF16 mixed precision. Optimization uses AdamW [ 60 ] , weight decay 0.01, an effective batch size of 8, and gradient norm clipping at 1.0. Diagonal loading adds 0.03 times the mean diagonal power to each covariance diagonal entry. Bearing soft labels have a Gaussian standard deviation of 5 ∘ 5^{\circ} . Frequency soft peaks have a standard deviation of 1 Hz and are truncated at ± 2 \pm 2 Hz. Bins with soft targets above 0.01 are positive; the remaining bins are negative. The frequency loss uses β − = 2 \beta_{-}=2 and β h = 0.5 \beta_{h}=0.5 . For each observation, the hard-negative term averages the losses of the 32 highest-scoring negative bins, or all negative bins when fewer are available. These settings apply to both frequency heads. VI-A 2 Training Protocol After acoustic pretraining, the encoder is frozen. Language adaptation proceeds sequentially: one epoch for detection and bearing, two epochs for frequency prediction, and one epoch for SNR, horizontal range, and explanation text. Environmental text and bathymetry are added in the last step. VI-B Evaluation Protocol Data partitions follow Section V . Encoder validation uses a separately trained model for each background, with 96 target-present observations at − 30 -30 dB and 96 target-absent observations. The test partition contains disjoint source realizations and was inspected during development. The reported evaluation uses fixed model parameters and decision rules. There are 288 target-present observations at each of 0, − 10 -10 , − 20 -20 , − 25 -25 , and − 30 -30 dB, together with 288 shared target-absent observations. VI-B 3 Frequency Outputs The language hidden-state readout assigns a score to each frequency bin. Local maxima with sigmoid scores of at least 0.5 are retained, followed by suppression that enforces a separation greater than 2 Hz. For each observation, evaluated frequency candidates are processed in increasing frequency order and paired with the nearest unmatched reference within ± 2 \pm 2 Hz. Each candidate and reference can be used once. Numerical coverage is reported with each result. Schema validity checks the four scalar field types and the target-absent null rules defined in Section IV-D . VI-C Acoustic Representation Performance Table V reports encoder validation at − 30 -30 dB. The white-noise model detects 96 of 96 targets, and the mixed-background model detects 95 of 96. Both produce zero false alarms in 96 target-absent observations. Bearing MAE is 1.26 ∘ 1.26^{\circ} and 0.22 ∘ 0.22^{\circ} , respectively. Frequency F1 is 96.72% and 81.79%. These results describe separately trained encoders evaluated on different background datasets. VI-D Detection and Structured Reports VI-D 1 Physical Output Performance Table VI reports system evaluation under the mixed background. Target presence, bearing, SNR, and range are generated as structured fields; frequencies are read from the language hidden state. The evaluated system detects all 1,440 targets and produces zero false alarms among 288 background observations. Overall bearing MAE is 0.208 ∘ 0.208^{\circ} , increasing to 0.521 ∘ 0.521^{\circ} at − 30 -30 dB. Of the 1,440 bearing outputs, 1,420 equal the reference angle and 20 differ by 15 ∘ 15^{\circ} . These errors are measured on the 24 tested bearings. Frequency precision, recall, and F1 are 67.11%, 43.95%, and 53.11%, respectively. The system matches 10,927 of 24,862 reference lines. Reference-line counts decrease from 6,086 at 0 dB to 3,424 at − 30 -30 dB because annotation depends on the received spectrum. The increase in F1 at lower SNR therefore involves different reference sets. Overall SNR MAE is 0.632 dB, with the largest error at − 10 -10 dB. Horizontal-range MAE is 8.803 km. A constant predictor equal to the training median of 7.5 km yields a lower MAE of 6.753 km on the same 1,439 observations. Per-record SNR scaling and common RMS normalization remove absolute received level as a unique range cue; any useful range information must remain in relative spatial or propagation structure. VI-D 2 Structured-Report Examples The four scalar fields satisfy the joint type and null-value rules in 1,727 of 1,728 reports. One target-present observation at − 30 -30 dB has null SNR and range, while its detection, bearing, and frequency outputs remain available. In Fig. 5 , each displayed pair contains a value and its support score. Scalar scores are geometric means of the conditional probabilities of the tokens forming the reported field value. Frequency scores are the sigmoid outputs at the corresponding bins. Scores are truncated to two decimal places for display. The positive example retains all 23 frequency candidates. Six of the ten highest-scoring candidates lie within ± 2 \pm 2 Hz of integer multiples of a possible 12.2 Hz fundamental, corresponding to a repetition period of approximately 82 ms. The illustrative explanation relates this pattern to a possible periodic source component and lists the other four candidates separately. The predicted bearing of 60 ∘ 60^{\circ} agrees with the reference, whereas the predicted range of 10 km differs from the reference range of 25 km. The report lists the predicted attributes and the proposed spectral relation for inspection.
