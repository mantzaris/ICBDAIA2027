III-A Feature–Evidence–Decision Chain The UPD framework organizes underwater acoustic sensing around an ordered feature–evidence–decision chain, ℱ = ϕ ⁡ ( X ac ) , ℰ = g ⁡ ( ℱ , 𝒫 ) , 𝒟 = h ⁡ ( ℰ ) , \mathcal{F}=\phi(X_{\mathrm{ac}}),\qquad\mathcal{E}=g(\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}),\qquad\mathcal{D}=h(\mathcal{E}), (1) where X ac X_{\mathrm{ac}} denotes acoustic observations, 𝒫 \mathcal{P} the embedded textual physical priors, ϕ \phi the physics-aware acoustic encoder, and g g , h h the evidence-forming and decision-making processes realized by a shared LLM reasoning core. The decision 𝒟 \mathcal{D} is conditioned on the stated evidence ℰ \mathcal{E} rather than directly on the features ℱ \mathcal{F} , distinguishing this chain from post-hoc explanation schemes. Two principles govern the design: physical grounding at every stage , ensuring that features correspond to identifiable acoustic structures rather than opaque activations, and a shared semantic space in which acoustic signals, environmental imagery, and textual priors are aligned into the LLM embedding space. III-B Multimodal Input Layer The input layer accommodates heterogeneous information without architectural modification: (i) acoustic modality —waveform-level observations from single or multiple hydrophones, possibly preprocessed by beamforming; (ii) environmental modality —sound speed profiles, bathymetric charts, sea state, and seabed properties, expressed as imagery or structured parameters; and (iii) textual modality —physical priors and expert knowledge in natural language. New sources are incorporated by adding encoders or extending the textual context. III-D From Recognition to Detection: What Changes Detection and recognition are two instantiations of the same chain, sharing a common semantic core but differing in four key respects [ 1 ] . (i) Operating conditions: recognition typically operates on target-bearing segments with relatively favorable SNR and well-defined physical priors, whereas detection must continuously process raw observations in which a target may be deeply buried in noise. (ii) Nature of input: recognition primarily uses single-channel, target-centered recordings, whereas detection processes multichannel array signals over extended durations and provides spatial information, such as target bearing, in addition to target-presence decisions. (iii) Physical front-end integration: detection relies more heavily on parameterized physical processing, including array processing, coherent integration, and Doppler compensation, incorporated before or within the encoder. (iv) Role of the environment: in recognition, environmental priors help explain how propagation shapes observed acoustic features; in detection, environmental modeling must also anticipate the effects of the noise field, fading, and channel-induced distortion on potential target signatures, thereby supporting reliable detection under adverse acoustic conditions.
