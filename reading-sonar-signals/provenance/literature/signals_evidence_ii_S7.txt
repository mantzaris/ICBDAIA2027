VII Conclusion In this paper, we have developed a physics-guided audio-language approach for underwater passive target detection, building on the unified framework introduced in Part I. The proposed acoustic encoder combines spatial, temporal, and spectral information from multichannel observations, using directional features to guide the extraction of weak acoustic structures. These representations are aligned with a pretrained language model and supplemented with environmental information to produce detection descriptions containing target-presence decisions and associated physical attributes. A physics-parameterized simulation pipeline provides multichannel recordings with traceable supervision, supporting both acoustic representation learning and language adaptation. Experiments with simulated targets under white and mixed backgrounds provide initial evidence for the feasibility of the proposed approach to passive acoustic detection. Together with the recognition study in Part I, this work extends the framework toward a more complete account of underwater acoustic targets, from detecting their presence to describing their characteristics. Future work may examine the approach under changing propagation conditions, diverse sensor configurations, and continuous observations of moving targets, including validation with field recordings. It will also investigate how generated descriptions relate to the acoustic information underlying detection decisions.
