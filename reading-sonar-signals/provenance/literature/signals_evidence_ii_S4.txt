IV Detection Instantiation: Physics-Guided Acoustic Encoding UPD estimates target presence, bearing, and spectral-line locations from synchronized array recordings. Its branches are named by their tasks: bearing estimation (DOA), target detection (DET), and spectral-line detection (LINE). Directional features from DOA guide feature aggregation in DET and LINE. The resulting acoustic representations and environmental inputs are aligned with a language model to produce structured reports. IV-A Physical Principles of Passive Detection Passive detection must distinguish weak target structure from background fluctuations while the source bearing and spectral lines are unknown. A local energy peak at an individual sensor provides limited evidence. The array and the observation duration provide additional information through spatial relationships and temporal and spectral structure. Let M a M_{\mathrm{a}} be the number of sensors, f f the frequency, and θ 0 \theta_{0} the bearing of a locally planar target component. Let 𝒙 ⁡ ( f ) ∈ ℂ M a \bm{x}(f)\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\mathrm{a}}} be the array spectrum, s ⁡ ( f ) s(f) the target’s complex spectral amplitude, and 𝒏 ⁡ ( f ) ∈ ℂ M a \bm{n}(f)\in\mathbb{C}^{M_{\mathrm{a}}} the background. The steering vector 𝒂 ⁡ ( f , θ 0 ) \bm{a}(f,\theta_{0}) depends on relative sensor positions, sound speed, and bearing. Under a narrowband approximation, 𝒙 ⁡ ( f ) = 𝒂 ⁡ ( f , θ 0 ) ​ s ​ ( f ) + 𝒏 ⁡ ( f ) . \bm{x}(f)=\bm{a}(f,\theta_{0})s(f)+\bm{n}(f). (5) The target-absent case has s ⁡ ( f ) = 0 s(f)=0 . When a target is present, its propagation delays produce phase relationships across sensors. Spatial covariance retains these relationships, and a directional spectrum evaluates candidate bearings. The full angular profile supplies directional information for aggregating acoustic features. A tonal component that is approximately stationary within an analysis window concentrates energy near its frequency. Short Fourier analysis windows retain local time-frequency variation; longer windows provide a finer spectral grid. Successive observation windows provide repeated views of the signal. Their ordered features retain temporal changes, while the means and standard deviations of fine-spectral features summarize average strength and variability. Directional information guides the aggregation of these complementary features for detection and line prediction. Independent linear maps project the three task summaries and the 𝐃 \mathbf{D} , 𝐓 ac \mathbf{T}_{\mathrm{ac}} , and 𝐅 \mathbf{F} sequences into the language-model embedding space. Each task summary occupies one input position. Group embeddings identify the source of local acoustic tokens, and within-group positional encodings retain their order. Bathymetry is supplied as a numerical depth matrix. After spatial pooling and normalization, local depth patches and their center coordinates are mapped to environmental tokens by a multilayer perceptron. Task instructions, sound speed, receiver coordinates, and array geometry are encoded as text. One additional frequency-readout position, derived from 𝐜 freq \mathbf{c}_{\mathrm{freq}} , is appended after the multimodal inputs. Its causal attention can access the complete input context. The structured answer follows this position. Two-stage adaptation. The first stage learns the acoustic encoder and task summaries with Eq. ( 17 ). The second stage freezes the acoustic encoder and base language-model parameters while training the input projections, bathymetric encoder, low-rank adaptation (LoRA) modules, and final frequency readout. LoRA is applied to the language model’s query and value projections [ 56 ] . Section VI-A specifies the task sequence. Language and frequency supervision. Each adaptation step uses the supervision assigned to its tasks. Structured output. The language model generates target presence, bearing, horizontal range, and received SNR. The frequency head produces independent sigmoid scores. Thresholding, local-peak selection, and minimum-separation suppression yield a variable-length line list, with each frequency paired with its sigmoid score. The scalar fields, frequency list, and explanatory text form the structured report. Target-absent reports use an empty frequency list and null physical attributes.
