IV Recognition Instantiation: Physics-Aware Encoding In the remainder of this paper, we instantiate the framework of Section III for the recognition task. The instantiation follows the three encoding rules established in Section 3.3: complementary representations are tokenized independently (Section 4.1), the encoder is supervised by physics-oriented analysis tasks rather than vessel identity alone (Sections 4.2–4.3), and the resulting tokens are aligned with the reasoning core under an answer-only objective (Section 4.4). The resulting acoustic encoder is termed the Underwater acoustic Physics-aware Transformer (UPT), and the complete recognition system, which couples UPT with a pretrained LLM, is illustrated in Fig. 3 . IV-B Task-Specific Feature Attention Following the physics-oriented supervision rule, UPT is organized around four physics analysis tasks:Shaft Frequency Estimation (SFE), Shaft Trend Estimation (STE), Blade-Passing Frequency Estimation (BPFE), and Machinery Line Detection (MLD), whose physical labels are derived from the simulation parameters (Section 5). The three representations contribute unequally to these tasks: the line spectrum is informative for SFE and MLD, whereas the DEMON spectrum is essential for STE and BPFE. A task-specific feature attention module is therefore employed to adaptively weight the representations for each task. IV-C Task-Disentangled Branches To prevent the physical semantics of different tasks from interfering with one another, the information flow is separated into four task-specific branches. In each branch, the shared acoustic tokens are weighted by the corresponding task-specific attention module; four learnable CLS tokens are then prepended to the weighted sequence and processed by a shared Transformer encoder consisting of 12 blocks, initialized with pretrained AudioMAE weights. The resulting token representations are projected into a task-specific subspace through an independent linear projection layer, which reduces the embedding dimension from D D to D / 4 D/4 ; since all branches preserve the same token ordering, their projected representations are concatenated along the feature dimension, yielding unified token sequences with the original dimension D D . This design allows the four physical semantics to be disentangled in dedicated subspaces while keeping the backbone shared. During UPT training, the projected CLS representations of each branch are averaged and fed into the corresponding prediction head. The task-specific attention modules, projection layers, and prediction heads are optimized by their corresponding task losses, while the shared patch embedding modules and Transformer encoder are jointly optimized through the combined objective. This design enables UPT to learn task-specialized representations while exploiting shared acoustic information across the four physical analysis tasks. By grounding representation learning in explicit physical tasks rather than vessel identity, UPT captures how a vessel sounds—its propulsion and machinery behavior—thereby providing the physically grounded features ℱ \mathcal{F} in Eq. (1). IV-D Alignment and Answer-Only Adaptation The unified token representations produced by UPT, comprising the four task CLS tokens and the acoustic tokens, are projected into the embedding space of a pretrained LLM (Qwen3-4B-Instruct) through a learnable projection module, and are concatenated with the embeddings of the textual query as in Eq. (2). Following the adaptation strategy of Section 3.4, LoRA modules are inserted into the LLM, while all remaining pretrained parameters are kept frozen; only the newly introduced UPT modules, the acoustic projection, and the LoRA adapters are optimized. This configuration preserves the pretrained knowledge and reasoning capability of the LLM and confines the learning burden to the acoustic-to-semantic mapping, which is essential given the limited scale of underwater acoustic data. Training follows the answer-only objective of Eq. (3): losses are computed exclusively over the answer tokens of the structured description, while the query and input portions are masked out. As analyzed in Section 3.4, this objective directs the learning signal toward evidence formation and decision-making by encouraging the model to extract physical attributes from acoustic features and organize them into structured descriptions, rather than reproducing the input or memorizing input–label associations. The two-stage training protocol first optimizes UPT using Eq. ( 13 ) and then optimizes the complete framework using Eq. (3), as detailed in Section 6.1.
