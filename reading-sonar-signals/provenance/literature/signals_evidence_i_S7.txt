VII Discussion and Conclusion VII-A Discussion VII-A 1 From description to evidence. A natural question is whether a generated description, however accurate, constitutes genuine evidence rather than fluent rationalization. The design of UPD addresses this concern at three levels. Structurally, the fixed field order enforces that physical attributes are reported before and alongside decisions, making the description an inspectable intermediate rather than a post-hoc justification. Physically, every reported attribute is grounded in synthesis parameters through the traceable data engine, so the correctness of each evidence statement can be verified field by field, as our attribute-level protocol does. Behaviorally, the task-specific attention coefficients offer an additional window into which physical representations the system relies upon for each task. VII-A 2 Perspectives. Several directions follow naturally from this work. First, while the present study verifies the correctness of evidence field by field, establishing its causal role —whether decisions change when the cited acoustic structures are removed—calls for intervention-based analysis, which would complete the validation of the feature–evidence–decision chain. Second, transferring the framework to field recordings is a promising next step: the physics-parameterized data engine can serve as a pretraining source for measured datasets such as ShipsEar and DeepShip, where the structured-description interface may further enable physically grounded annotation of real-world data. Third, augmenting each reported attribute with a calibrated confidence estimate would allow downstream users, whether human operators or cooperating sensing nodes, to weigh evidence according to its reliability. Finally, the open input layer of the framework invites richer physical priors—sound speed profiles, bathymetric information, and operational context—to be incorporated through the textual channel, moving toward recognition that reasons about the environment as an experienced analyst would. VII-B Conclusion We have presented a from-signals-to-evidence framework that couples physics-aware acoustic encoding with a pretrained large language model to advance underwater acoustic recognition from hard labels toward interpretable, evidence-grounded descriptions. As the first part of a two-part series, this paper established the general framework—organized around a feature–evidence–decision chain with a shared LLM reasoning core—and instantiated it for the recognition task through the Underwater acoustic Physics-aware Transformer, which learns task-disentangled physical representations from complementary acoustic representations. A physics-parameterized data engine was developed to supply traceable, dual-granularity supervision, and an attribute-level evaluation protocol was proposed to assess the physical correctness of generated descriptions. Experiments on simulated recordings of four vessels demonstrated accurate vessel recognition together with reliable estimation of shaft-frequency, blade-passing, and machinery-related physical attributes, validating the feasibility of evidence-grounded underwater acoustic recognition. The companion Part II will instantiate the same framework for target detection under low-SNR conditions, where multi-channel array processing and environment prediction are developed in depth. We hope this work provides a step toward underwater acoustic systems whose conclusions can be inspected, verified, and trusted.
